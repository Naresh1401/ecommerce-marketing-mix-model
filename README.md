# 📊 E-Commerce Marketing Mix Model

> **Moving beyond last-click attribution to understand true channel contribution.**

Last-click attribution gives all the credit to the final touchpoint before a purchase — meaning Google Shopping looks like a hero while TV, influencers, and email campaigns that *built the demand* get nothing. Marketing Mix Modelling (MMM) fixes this by measuring the **actual causal contribution** of every channel, accounting for the "advertising hangover" (adstock), diminishing returns (saturation), promotions, and seasonality.

This project builds a full Bayesian MMM for a direct-to-consumer e-commerce brand, estimates channel ROI, and recommends the optimal budget reallocation for maximum revenue.

### 2. Run the full pipeline

```bash

```

This will:
1. Generate 156 weeks of synthetic e-commerce data
2. Apply adstock & saturation transforms
3. Fit the Bayesian MMM (2 chains × 2,000 draws — takes ~15–30 min)
4. Run revenue decomposition & channel ROI analysis
5. Optimise the quarterly budget allocation
6. Save all artefacts to `artefacts/`

### Embedded patterns

- **Black Friday (week 47):** ~4× revenue spike, 3× media spend
- **January slump:** -30% revenue (post-holiday hangover)
- **Influencer lag:** Content takes ~2 weeks to gain traction (adstock decay = 0.6)
- **Email efficiency:** Near-zero carryover but highest direct ROI (owned channel)
- **TV persistence:** Long memory (decay = 0.85) but saturates fastest

## 🔬 Methodology

### Media Transformations

**Adstock (the "advertising hangover"):** When you see a TV ad on Monday, you don't forget it by Tuesday. Adstock captures this memory effect — each week's effective spend is a blend of this week's actual spend plus a decaying carryover from previous weeks. TV has the longest hangover (decay = 0.85, meaning 85% carries over), while search is nearly instant (decay = 0.2).

**Saturation (diminishing returns):** The first £10K you spend on Facebook reaches eager audiences. The next £10K reaches less interested people. Eventually you're paying premium prices for marginal eyeballs. The Hill saturation function captures this S-curve — rapid returns at low spend, flattening at high spend.

**Promotion multiplier:** Sales events don't just add revenue — they multiply the effectiveness of everything else. A 25% discount during Black Friday amplifies the pull from every channel.

### Bayesian MMM

The model estimates:

```
Revenue = Baseline + Σ(Channel Contributions) + Promo Uplift + Seasonality + Noise
```

Where each channel contribution = `β × Saturate(Adstock(Spend))`.

**Why Bayesian?** Because we get uncertainty for free. Instead of a single ROI number, we get a full distribution — "email ROI is probably between 3.5 and 5.2, with a best guess of 4.2." This matters when you're reallocating real budgets.

**Priors incorporate business knowledge:**
- Email gets a high-ROI prior (it's an owned channel with near-zero marginal cost)
- TV gets a lower-ROI but high-reach prior (brand building, not direct response)
- Influencer gets an uncertain prior (highly variable by creator)

**Seasonality** is modelled with Fourier series (2 harmonics) capturing annual patterns like the Q4 peak and January dip.

## 📈 Key Findings

### Channel Contribution & ROI

| Channel       | Contribution % | ROI (Mean) | 95% Credible Interval | vs Current Allocation |
|--------------|---------------|------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Email         | ~28%          | ~4.2       | [3.5, 5.2]           | **Underinvested** ✅   |
| Paid Search   | ~18%          | ~2.5       | [1.9, 3.2]           | Roughly right         |
| Paid Social   | ~15%          | ~1.8       | [1.3, 2.4]           | Slightly underinvested|
| Influencer    | ~8%           | ~1.5       | [0.8, 2.3]           | Uncertain — test more |
| TV            | ~22%          | ~0.9       | [0.5, 1.3]           | **Overinvested** 🔴    |
| Affiliate     | ~6%           | ~2.0       | [1.4, 2.7]           | Roughly right         |
| OOH           | ~3%           | ~0.7       | [0.3, 1.1]           | **Overinvested** 🔴    |

> *Exact numbers depend on the MCMC run. Values above are representative.*

### Black Friday Insight

Black Friday generates ~4× normal weekly revenue, but how much is truly incremental?

- **~40-50% is organic demand** — customers would have bought anyway (pent-up demand, payday timing)
- **~35-40% is marketing-driven** — the 3× spend increase genuinely pulled forward purchases
- **~10-15% is promo-driven** — the discount depth (25-40%) converted fence-sitters

**Implication:** The brand is likely *over-attributing* Black Friday success to marketing. Consider testing a lower-spend BF strategy for one product category.

### Budget Reallocation Recommendation

For a £500K quarterly budget, the optimizer recommends:

| Shift | Channel | Current → Optimal |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| +£30K | Email | Scale owned channel campaigns |
| +£20K | Paid Social | More headroom before saturation |
| +£10K | Paid Search | High-intent, still efficient |
| -£35K | TV | Deep into diminishing returns |
| -£15K | OOH | Low measurable ROI |
| -£10K | Influencer | Hold until measurement improves |

**Expected revenue uplift: +6–10%** versus current allocation (quarter-dependent).

## ⚠️ Caveats & Limitations

1. **Synthetic data** — Results illustrate the methodology; real-world ROI would differ.
2. **Stable competitive environment assumed** — The model doesn't account for a competitor suddenly doubling their ad spend.
3. **Organic/SEO excluded** — Brand search (google_trends_index) is included as a control but organic acquisition is baked into the baseline.
4. **No cross-channel interactions** — TV + social may be greater than the sum of parts (halo effects), but this model treats channels independently.
5. **Aggregate weekly data** — Cannot capture within-week dynamics (e.g., weekend vs weekday spend efficiency).
6. **Priors matter** — The Bayesian priors encode assumptions. Different priors → different posteriors, especially with only 156 data points.
